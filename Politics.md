# 1. Farfield Interstellar
 
The corporation that founded Nova Reach and its branch systems. Full name Farfield Interstellar; abbreviated Farfield or FI in both formal and casual use.
 
<a name="2"></a>
## 2 Power nodes (network-wide, post-severance relevance)
 
- Gate controllers — control inter-system access, can toll or deny passage.
- Resource monopolists — e.g. the Ereshkigal phosphorus chokepoint; starvation leverage over agriculture-dependent colonies.
- Local law enforcement — the only armed force in the network; no corporate military exists. Must be mobilized rapidly if force is needed.
- Wormhole engineers — control the physical possibility of the network's future (repair, potential new gates).
- Hazard/maintenance crews — keep existing gates from collapsing.
<a name="3"></a>
## 3 Post-severance dynamics (established direction, not yet detailed)
 
- The pre-severance steady state (Year 36–92) is stable, extractive, universally resented, and completely functional. Farfield's amoral efficiency is normalized; resentment calcifies into resignation. Everyone knows the system is evil; nobody expects it to change.
- Year 92: The severance is exogenous, sudden, a random catastrophe — not the inevitable result of building tensions. It exposes a system that was only ever held together by ruthless efficiency and lack of alternatives.
- Post-severance, Nova Reach attempts to maintain legitimacy and provide for the branch systems, and partially fails due to internal chaos and limited capacity — compounded by the comm-delay problem detailed in [§5](#5): central coordination across 20-minute-to-hour-plus one-way lags was already strained before the collapse, and becomes unworkable during it. All accumulated resentment erupts because the system holding it in place is gone.
- Likely outcome: one or two large coalitions plus several smaller, fractured systems under extreme governance (feudal, dictatorial, etc.).
- A charismatic leader (gender under consideration) leads early peaceful rebuilding efforts, is later assassinated, and becomes a martyr/symbol of hope for stability. *(See [People §1](People.md#1) for the now-locked leadership roster — this figure is most likely Halvard Deschamps; final confirmation of the martyr/Deschamps link is still an open cross-reference, see Open Questions.)*
- Government type per colony depends on: resource self-sufficiency, population ideology mix, dependency on Nova Reach, and (newly established) how tightly each settlement is coupled to its Safford station by communication lag — see [§5](#5).
- Inspirations: Soviet collapse (messy, multi-decade, competing factions, no clean resolution), early American frontier, COVID-era supply chain fragility.

<a name="4"></a>
# 4. Post-Collapse Governance Structures
 
By the time the network stabilizes into an uneasy equilibrium, each system has developed distinct governance appropriate to its ideological composition, resources, and dependency relationships.
 
<a name="4.1"></a>
## 4.1 Emergent Local Leadership, Safford's Isolation, and the Negotiation Period
 
The governance outcomes in [§4.2](#4.2)–11.4 aren't imposed by Nova Reach or dictated by geography alone — they're the settled residue of a real negotiation, fought and bargained over during the chaotic months after Year 92, between local leaders who emerge on the ground and a Nova Reach that suddenly needs their cooperation far more than it can compel it.
 
### Safford is Nova Reach territory, not a local settlement
 
Per [Infrastructure §2](Infrastructure.md#2), every Safford wing is one continuous structure, physically and administratively part of Nova Reach's network regardless of which system it sits in. This matters for governance: Safford's staff and administrators are Nova Reach personnel, culturally and politically isolated from the planet or moon they orbit — closer in spirit to a naval base than to a colony. They do not compete for local leadership, and their proximity to a given settlement (Boann's co-orbital closeness in Danu; Ereshkigal's near-neighbor distance in Enlil) does not translate into political alignment with it.
 
This makes Safford, in every system, the thing local leadership negotiates *over* rather than a party *at* the table. Local leaders need Safford — it is the only way in or out ([§2](#2), gate control) — and Nova Reach needs local leaders' cooperation (production, no revolt) once its own coercive capacity is exposed as thin ([§2](#2) notes no corporate military exists). A Safford wing can be besieged, cut off, or held as a bargaining chip by a system's population, as Danu does at Boann; it is never itself a candidate for local rule. Any Safford personnel who do go local — sympathizing with, aiding, or defecting to the population they're stationed beside — read as individual betrayals precisely because they cut against this structural isolation, not as a natural drift.
 
### One leader per settlement, then a spokesperson
 
In the vacuum after Year 92, each system's real settlements — not Safford — throw up their own local leadership by necessity, usually built on whoever already held practical authority (a senior administrator, an engineer, a hazard-crew chief) before the crisis forced the question. Where a system has more than one settlement, these leaders then have to deal with each other as well as with Nova Reach, and one of them typically emerges as the system's spokesperson for external negotiation — not by formal design, but because someone has to be the voice Nova Reach actually talks to.
 
- **Danu — clean hierarchy.** Lugh and Boann are the system's two real settlements. Per [§5.1](#5.1), Lugh's agricultural operation was already forced into independent local decision-making well before the collapse, so Lugh's leader is the dominant figure by Year 92 and the natural organizer of the Boann water seizure ([§4.2](#4.2), [History §3.1](History.md#3.1)). Boann, tightly coupled to Safford and comparatively weak on its own, is absorbed into Lugh's authority rather than producing a rival leader. Lugh's leader becomes spokesperson essentially by default — they were already governing independently before anyone had to formalize it.
- **Obatala — contested, but tilted toward Yemoja.** Yemoja and Olokun are the real settlements. Following Safford's relocation to co-orbit Olokun ([Cosmology §4.2](Cosmology.md#4.2)), Yemoja is now the genuinely distant, slow-coupled world — forced into real local decision-making the way Lugh was, and producing the stronger, more independent leader as a result. Olokun, by contrast, is Nova Reach's own post-collapse investment (reactors, ice-shipping contracts, hydroponic domes, all built and held as corporate assets) sitting in near-instant comm contact with Safford — its administration answers to Nova Reach in something close to real time, leaving little room for an independent leader to emerge there. The spokesperson role still ends up genuinely negotiated rather than simply defaulting to Yemoja's leader, since Olokun's controllers retain real institutional leverage even without local political weight — a compromise position that later gets formalized and sold as the presidential/sectoral-autonomy structure in [§4.5](#4.5), rather than being its actual origin.
- **Enlil — uncontested by necessity.** Ereshkigal is the only real settlement, small and harsh enough ([Cosmology §4.3](Cosmology.md#4.3), [§4.4](#4.4)) that one administrator becomes the obvious point of contact almost by default, much as a single factory director becomes a town's de facto authority once distant central oversight loosens. That leader negotiates from real weakness, however, because Safford sits close enough ([Cosmology §4.3](Cosmology.md#4.3), [§4.4](#4.4)) that Nova Reach retains near-real-time reach and genuine leverage — Ereshkigal's leadership is uncontested locally but constrained externally in a way Danu's and Obatala's are not.
### Negotiation and goodwill
 
Once local leadership exists, a period of open negotiation follows — overlapping with, not strictly sequential to, the military arc in [History §3](History.md#3). What each system ends up with (Danu's cold independence, Obatala's negotiated sectoral autonomy, Enlil's tight subordination) is not a deterministic output of geography or resources alone; it's shaped by concrete choices made by specific people during Year 92, tracked here loosely as goodwill:
 
- Whether a local leader shared scarce resources across settlements or hoarded them.
- Whether Nova Reach-aligned personnel stranded in local territory (including stranded or besieged Safford staff) were protected or left to suffer.
- Whether the chaos was used to settle old scores unrelated to the crisis itself.
- Whether Nova Reach's Phase 1 diplomatic outreach ([History §3.1](History.md#3.1)) arrived with something real to offer, or as empty, comm-lag-stale noise.
Goodwill is tracked per settlement during the negotiation window itself — a Boann leader who never fired a shot may bank differently than the Lugh leader who organized the seizure, even though both end up under the same flag — and then flattens into a single system-level relationship once a spokesperson structure solidifies, producing the three system-level postures in [§4.2](#4.2)–11.4. This leaves room for internal friction that outlives the negotiation: a settlement leader who resents that a harder line taken elsewhere in their own system spent goodwill they didn't personally choose to spend.
 
### The contested lesson
 
Nova Reach's own leadership does not draw a single, unanimous lesson from any of this — see [§3](#3) and [History §3](History.md#3) for the internal factional split between administrators who conclude Phase 1 was too soft, Phase 2 was too harsh, distance made central control structurally impossible regardless of tone, or that Danu was simply an exceptional case that doesn't generalize. Which reading prevails, if any single one does, tends to track whichever faction already controlled policy toward a given system — Enlil's near-real-time reach makes the "control still works" reading look locally correct almost regardless of merit, while Obatala's genuine distance makes the "distance defeats control" reading look correct there. Geography quietly validates whoever was already in charge, rather than any faction actually winning the argument on its merits.
 
<a name="4.2"></a>
## 4.2 The Danu Democratic Republic
 
Danu achieves functional independence through adaptation and successful defense against Nova Reach military incursions. Its governance reflects the idealist and worker base that seized control:
 
- Democratic republic with elected leadership, typically the charismatic leader who organized the initial water seizure
- Governance by councils representing major sectors (agriculture, security, inter-system relations)
- Legitimacy derived from popular mandate and successful resistance
- Internally messy and debate-driven, but politically stable because it reflects genuine popular consent
- Independent but resource-constrained: relies on closed-loop phosphorus recycling and hydrogen-oxygen rockets
<a name="4.3"></a>
## 4.3 The Farfield Technocratic Zone
 
Nova Reach and its dependent systems (Enlil, Obatala) coalesce into a continuation of corporate rule under a new name. Governance is centralized, efficient, and closed to popular input:
 
- Leadership is technocratic oligarchy: the highest-ranking administrator at the collapse becomes first leader by default
- Subsequent leaders are either appointed by the previous leader or agreed upon by the inner council of senior engineers and administrators
- No popular elections, but justified by technical expertise: "these people know how to run the gates and trelium production"
- Controls gate access, trelium production, and overall inter-system trade
- Internally stable but authoritarian
<a name="4.4"></a>
## 4.4 Enlil (within Farfield Technocratic Zone)
 
- Remains under corporate-style autocracy, dependent on food imports from Farfield-controlled sources
- Leadership is appointed by Nova Reach or locally agreed among the small population of administrators
- Harsh conditions and small population (2,000–4,000) preclude democratic development
- Population is rotational; few develop long-term political attachments
- Enlil's tight Safford–Ereshkigal comm coupling (~35 second round-trip, [Infrastructure §5](Infrastructure.md#5)) makes it the one branch system where Nova Reach can exercise genuinely real-time central control — reinforcing rather than straining its subordinate status.
<a name="4.5"></a>
## 4.5 Obatala (within Farfield Technocratic Zone)
 
Develops a presidential system with autonomous sectors, similar in structure to Earth governments with separation of powers:
 
- Strong local president/coordinator with significant executive authority, but not absolute rule
- Critical sectors operate semi-independently with their own leadership:
- Agricultural sector (Yemoja's ocean-world food production): substantially autonomous production and distribution decisions, run by Yemoja's own local leadership
- Hydrocarbon/agricultural-buildout sector (Olokun's methane/ethane extraction and Nova Reach-funded hydroponics): remains a direct Nova Reach asset rather than a genuinely autonomous sector — its "seat at the table" is institutional leverage (control of reactors, ice shipments, and the post-collapse food-security investment), not independent local political weight
- Local governance and security: day-to-day administration
- Checks and balances: Yemoja's leadership can negotiate with the president, form coalitions, resist overreach; Olokun's administration negotiates from Nova Reach's side of the table rather than against it
- Reflects pragmatist ideology: people care about functional governance and sectoral autonomy more than ideological purity
- Sectoral autonomy is mechanically reinforced by geography, but only for Yemoja: its ~1–1.6 hour one-way comm lag to Safford ([Infrastructure §5](Infrastructure.md#5), following Safford's relocation to co-orbit Olokun) makes real-time central coordination impossible, so its operations necessarily run on local authority. Olokun, by contrast, sits in near-instant contact with Safford and is governed close to directly.
- Ultimately subordinate to Nova Reach's gate and trelium control, but Yemoja maintains substantial internal autonomy where Olokun does not
<a name="4.6"></a>
## 4.6 The Political Division: Two Space Nations
 
The two post-collapse political entities take their formal names directly from existing identities rather than inventing new ones:
 
- Farfield: the corporate-technocratic zone (Nova Reach, Enlil, Obatala) simply retains the name of the original corporation. No rebranding — the continuity of the name reflects the continuity of the hierarchy.
- Republic of Danu: the independent democratic zone takes its name from the star system itself, paired with "Republic" to reflect its elected government and worker/idealist origins.
The post-collapse network fractures into two distinct political entities:
 
- The Farfield Technocratic Zone: Nova Reach, Enlil, and Obatala under centralized corporate-style rule. Maintains the old hierarchy in new form. Controls gate infrastructure and trelium production.
- The Danu Democratic Republic: Independent, self-governing, ideologically committed to autonomy and worker representation. Achieves this through adaptation and successful military resistance.
The two entities maintain an uneasy peace: Danu is independent but resource-constrained; Farfield has structural advantages but cannot afford the cost of conquering Danu. Both prepare for potential future conflict while maintaining diplomatic relations and minimal trade.

# 5. Communication Delay & the Structure of Post-Collapse Authority
 
The comm-delay figures in [Infrastructure §5](Infrastructure.md#5) aren't just color — they mechanically shape why central authority succeeds or fails in each branch system, both before and after the severance.
 
<a name="5.1"></a>
## 5.1 Pre-collapse stratification
 
Once each branch station goes live (Danu: Year 19; Enlil: Year 22; Obatala: Year 30), every system splits into a fast-coupled cluster and a slow-coupled one:
 
- Danu: Safford and Boann (near-instant, co-orbital) versus Lugh (48–51 minutes one-way). Lugh's agricultural operation has no choice but to develop real local decision-making authority early — an hour-plus round trip makes gate-side micromanagement of daily operations unworkable.
- Obatala: following Safford's relocation to co-orbit Olokun ([Cosmology §4.2](Cosmology.md#4.2)), Safford and Olokun are near-instant, co-orbital neighbors — Olokun is managed close to directly, the way Boann is in Danu. Yemoja, by contrast, sits roughly 61–97 minutes one-way — functionally isolated in the same way Lugh is in Danu. During the mature-hub period (Year 30–92), this pushes Yemoja toward genuine local self-sufficiency and decision-making well before the collapse, while Olokun's post-collapse buildout ([Cosmology §4.2](Cosmology.md#4.2)) remains a directly-administered Nova Reach investment rather than an independent settlement.
- Enlil: Safford and Ereshkigal are practically neighbors (17–32 sec one-way). This is the only branch system where the gate station and the main settlement stay tightly coupled, reinforcing Enlil's character as a single, focused outpost rather than a distributed colony.
<a name="5.2"></a>
## 5.2 The severance and the collapse of central coordination
 
At the moment Sat-39 fails (Year 92), Nova Reach knows immediately. The branch systems learn nearly as fast via the gate-to-gate fiber threading ([Cosmology §1](Cosmology.md#1), [Infrastructure §5](Infrastructure.md#5)) — but a colony's internal population doesn't hear it from the gate directly. News has to propagate in-system at light speed on top of that: Danu's Safford learns close to instantly, but Lugh only hears from Safford 48–51 minutes later; Obatala's Yemoja stays in the dark for roughly an hour to an hour and a half.
 
More importantly, the three branch systems cannot coordinate with each other except by routing through Nova Reach — and Nova Reach is, at that exact moment, in crisis. This is the real mechanism behind the failure of Phase 1's cooperative-federation attempt ([History §3.1](History.md#3.1)): it is not simply that the conservative leader lacks the will or authority to hold the network together, but that hour-plus round-trip coordination with Lugh (and comparable lag with Yemoja) makes real-time crisis management structurally impossible, even for a competent, well-intentioned leader.
 
By Phase 2 ([History §3.2](History.md#3.2)), Danu's field commanders at Lugh are already making decisions on local authority by necessity, because Nova Reach's orders arrive too stale to be useful. Ambiguous or delayed directives read as hostile intent on both sides, which is exactly the kind of miscommunication that turns a tense standoff into an actual armed clash.
 
<a name="5.3"></a>
## 5.3 Why the post-collapse governance structures stick
 
The independent decision-making structures each system builds during the crisis don't get dismantled once the fighting stops — they become the actual shape of post-collapse governance, because the underlying comm-delay geography never changes:
 
- Danu's councils and elected leadership ([§4.2](#4.2)) formalize what Lugh's defenders were already doing out of necessity during Phase 2.
- Obatala's sectoral autonomy ([§4.5](#4.5)) is the peacetime version of Yemoja's forced self-sufficiency during the mature-hub period — Olokun, by contrast, never developed comparable independence, since Safford's relocation ([Cosmology §4.2](Cosmology.md#4.2)) kept it in near-continuous Nova Reach contact throughout.
- Enlil's continued subordination to Nova Reach ([§4.4](#4.4)) is the one case where central authority remains genuinely workable, because the comm lag never made it otherwise.
*Net effect: the severance and the resource/ideological pressures already established elsewhere in the bible (colonist strata and ideology in [Culture.md](./Culture.md), the extraction pipeline in [Economy.md](./Economy.md), power nodes and post-severance dynamics above, and the military arc in [History.md](./History.md)) remain the primary drivers of the post-collapse political split. Comm-delay geography is a reinforcing, load-bearing mechanism underneath that story, not a competing explanation for it — it's the reason central authority was already brittle before Year 92, and the reason each colony's improvised wartime autonomy hardens into permanent governance afterward.*
